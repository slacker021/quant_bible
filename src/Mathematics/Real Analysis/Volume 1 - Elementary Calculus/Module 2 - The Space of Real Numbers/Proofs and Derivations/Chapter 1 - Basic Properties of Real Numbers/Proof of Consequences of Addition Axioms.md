# Proof of Proposition 1
If $0_{1}$ and $0_{2}$ are both zeros in $\mathbb{R}$, then by the definition of zero, 
$$
0_{1} = 0_{1} + 0_{2} = 0_{2} + 0_{1} = 0_{2} \tag{1}
$$

---
# Proof of Proposition 2
If $x_{1}$ and $x_{2}$ are both negatives of $x \in \mathbb{R}$, then 
$$
x_{1} = x_{1} + 0 = x_{1} + (x + x_{2}) = (x_{1} + x) + x_{2} = 0 + x_{2} = x_{2} \tag{2}
$$

---
# Proof of Proposition 3
This follows from the existence and uniqueness of the negative of element $a \in \mathbb{R}$:
$$
\begin{align}
(a + x) & \iff ((x + a) + (-a) = b + (-a)) \iff \\
& \iff (x + (a + (-a)) = b + (-a)) \iff (x + 0 = b + (-a)) \iff \\
& \iff (x = b + (-a)) \tag{3}
\end{align}
$$
$$
\textbf{Q.E.D}
$$
