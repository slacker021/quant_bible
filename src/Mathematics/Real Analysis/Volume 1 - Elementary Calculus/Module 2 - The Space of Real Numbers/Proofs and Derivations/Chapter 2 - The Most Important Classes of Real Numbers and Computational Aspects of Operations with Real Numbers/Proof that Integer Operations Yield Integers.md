If $m$ and $n$ are integers, either one of these integers are zero, and then the sum $m + n$ equals the other number, so that $(m + n) \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $m \cdot n = 0 \in \mathbb{Z}$, or both numbers are non-zero. In the latter case, either $m,n \in \mathbb{N}$ and then $(m+n) \in \mathbb{N} \subset \mathbb{Z}$ and $(m \cdot n) \in \mathbb{N} \subset \mathbb{Z}$, or $(-m),-(n) \in \mathbb{N}$ and then $m \cdot n = ((-1)m)((-1)m) \in \mathbb{N}$ or $(-m),n \in \mathbb{N}$ and then $(-m \cdot n) \in \mathbb{N}$, that is $m \cdot n \in \mathbb{Z}$, or, finally, $m,-n \in \mathbb{N}$ and then $(-m \cdot n) \in \mathbb{N}$ and once again $m \cdot n \in \mathbb{Z}$.
$$
\textbf{Q.E.D}
