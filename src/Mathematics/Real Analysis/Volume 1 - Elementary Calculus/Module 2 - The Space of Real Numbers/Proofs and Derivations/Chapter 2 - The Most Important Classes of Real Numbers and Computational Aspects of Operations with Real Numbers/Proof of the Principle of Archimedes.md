Since $\mathbb{Z}$ [[Proof of the Bounds in the Natural Numbers and Integers|isn't bounded above,]] the set $\left\{  n \in \mathbb{Z} \ | \ \frac{x}{h} < n  \right\}$ is a nonempty subset of the integers that's bounded below. Then it contains a minimal element $k$, that is $(k - 1) \le x/h < k$. Since $h > 0$, these inequalities are equivalent to those given in the statement of the principle of Archimedes. The uniqueness of $k \in \mathbb{Z}$ satisfying these two inequalities follows from the uniqueness of the minimal element of a set of numbers.
